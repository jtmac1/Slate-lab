How this simulator works
Contest generator
Builds one lineup per contest entry. Players are drawn slot by slot from projected ownership raised to the chalk-pull power, with a salary floor and ceiling enforced as the lineup fills, and stack pull raising the odds that a quarterback is joined by their own receivers and a bring-back. Calibration rounds compare field exposure to projected ownership and re-weight until they agree.
Outcomes
Each player draws from a skewed distribution rather than a point estimate — lognormal with mean equal to the projection, calibrated from standard deviation where the source gives one, otherwise from the ceiling-to-projection ratio. Defenses use a normal floored at −4, since DK pays negative points for allowing 35+.
Correlation
Two engines. Pools of 60 or fewer players use a full pair-wise matrix, repaired to the nearest valid one by eigenvalue clipping — that is the editable grid on the Correlation tab, and it is exact. Larger pools (a full MLB or NFL main slate) use a factor model: a game factor, a team factor and an opposing-team loading per role.
Pre-contest sim
Scores the saved contest and your lineups on the same game outcome every iteration. Rank counts every contest entry above you and every one of your own lineups above you. Winnings split among exact duplicates, so a lineup the field also holds earns less.
Correlation lift
Your set's chance of landing at least one lineup in the top 1%, against what it would be if the lineups were independent. Negative means they win and lose together.

What this is not. The correlation values are hand-specified from published figures, not fitted to historical data, and the field model is a structured ownership draw, not a replay of how entrants actually build. Use it to test structure cheaply rather than as a replacement for a commercial simulator.
